18  River Flow Structure Centerline

Spatial & Geometric Definition

  • Geometry Type: Polyline (Flow_Structure_Centerline).

  • Spatial Delineation: Longitudinal linear crest axis of a riverine training dike, chevron, or rock closure structure.

  • Data Fields: Structure ID, structure class (wing dike, closing dam, chevron), crest elevation, top width, upstream azimuth, and baseline stationing.

Primary Restoration & Engineering Objectives

  • Alignment Guidance: Guides derrick barge positioning during underwater rock dumping operations.

  • Hydraulic Modeling Reference: Defines the exact mathematical weir boundary line in 2D and 3D hydrodynamic models (e.g., Adaptive Hydraulics - AdH, HEC-RAS 2D).

Typical Specifications & Geometric Properties

  • Survey Baseline: Linear axis delineating the centerline of stone placement; elevation profile reflects the exact design crest grade without sagging.

  • Coordinate Accuracy: Sub-decimeter horizontal precision tied into local State Plane coordinates.

Construction & Implementation Methodology

  • DGPS Marine Guidance Systems: Centerline profile strings loaded into onboard barge positioning systems (e.g., Trimble Marine Construction software) to ensure stone dumping remains centered within design tolerances (±1.0 ft).

Quality Control, Inspection & Long-Term Maintenance

  • Crest Alignment Verification: High-precision RTK or lidar scans along the exposed crest during low-flow river stages to confirm structure crest stability.

Spatial Feature Attribute

Definition

Riverine Flow Structure Line is a natural or man-made flow (or sediment) control structure in a water course or water body such as a dike or weir. This feature should not be used to model a levee. The line feature class is for features that are too small to be drawn as polygons and are better represented as lines.

Purpose

Riverine flow structures, also called “river training structures,” are man-made constructions designed to modify the flow of a river, typically to control erosion, manage sediment transport, improve navigation, protect riverbanks, and sometimes enhance aquatic habitats by influencing water flow dynamics within the river channel.

Applicability

Refined construction techniques improve habitat through site-specific modifications. Successfully combining a broad range of construction techniques with approaches that strive to use or mimic natural riverine processes provides benefits to the river at system, reach, pool, and local scales. Programs continually build upon lessons learned in constructing and managing prior projects. (UMRR Design Handbook, 2012)

Notes

Restoration features now have a Program_UID field to distinguish between NESP and UMRR features. Aliases without program identification will be created for any field that currently has UMRR in the name, so they can be used for NESP or UMRR data.

Attribute criteria for each field includes the following:

  • Attribute Status – defines whether the field requires a value or if it is optional based on available information.
  • Phases – indicates the earliest time in the project lifecycle values for this attribute are available.
  • Digitizing requirements – indicates when the field value should be entered in the attribute table.

Geometry Note: Points should have well top elevation as Z value if available.

RiverineFlowStructureLine

(line) – A natural or man-made flow (or sediment) control structure in a water course or water body such as a dike or weir. This feature should be used to represent the footprint of the feature.

Parameters

Program_UID

Designate program NESP or UMRR.

Project_ID (alias: PROJECT_ID)

Foreign key to the unique NESP/UMRR project ID.

Type (alias)

A broad classification used to group NESP/UMRR restoration features into categories of similar function.

Value Description
Shoreline Protection Measures taken to reduce erosion from banks and islands due to wind, wave, and ice action.
Channel Modifications Measures taken to train the main channel of a river and to modify secondary channels.

SubType (alias: Subtype)

A classification used to group NESP/UMRR restoration features into specific design feature categories.

Value Description
Rock groin Placement of rock extending from shore into shallow water, perpendicular to shore. Encourages deposition of sediment along the groin and erosion in the reach between two groins to form stable scallop shapes. Appropriate where wave action and littoral drift are dominant processes.
Rock vane Placement of rock extending from shore into water at angles from 45–60 degrees upstream, away from shore. Features a sloping top and functions to direct current away from shore; can also reduce littoral drift.
J-hook vane Placement of rock extending from shore into water at angles from 45–60 degrees upstream, away from shore, with a “J”-shaped hook whose outside curve faces upstream.
Cross vane U-shaped vane with either end keyed into banklines and sloping down to the top of the U pointing upstream, used to offset adverse effects of straight weirs and check dams.
Root-wad vane Placement of root wads extending from shore into water at angles from 45–60 degrees upstream, away from shore.
Spur dike River training structures generally constructed in a series perpendicular to shore to establish normal channel width, direct the axis of flow, promote scour and sediment deposition, and trap bedload to build banks.
L-head dike Training dike with a perpendicular dike structure attached at the channel end, creating an “L” shape; controls scour patterns at the riverward end of the training dike.
Alternating dike River training structures usually constructed in straight side channels to encourage development of sinuous flow.
Stepped-up dike Field of dikes that increase in elevation (generally ~2 ft from dike to dike) used to change morphology of natural alluvial waterways.
Chevron dike V- or U-shaped rock dike pointing upstream and unattached to the bankline, typically built above normal flat pool elevation to approximately a 2-year flood elevation; used in wider reaches to provide flow split and habitat and to provide dredged-material placement area.
Longitudinal peak stone toe Continuous stone dike keyed into the bank at both upstream and downstream ends, placed at the toe of an eroding bend bankline; may be placed slightly away from the bank to smooth alignment through the bend.
Bendway weir Low-level, completely submerged rock structure placed on the outside bankline of a river bend and angled upstream toward flow, extending into the navigation channel, used to prevent excess channel deepening and reduce erosion on the outside of the bendway.
W-weir Placement of rock in a W shape in the channel, center point facing downstream; each end is tied into the bank at bankfull elevation and slopes down to the two upstream points and up slightly to the center point, providing bed control and enhanced fish habitat.
Closure structure Rock, earth, or wood structures constructed across secondary channels to reduce floodplain conveyance and increase main channel depths; can be submerged or emerged.
Rock-Log structure Economical alternative to offshore rock mounds where ice impacts are minimal; piles of rock anchor both ends of logs.
Wood pile structure Logs driven into the river bed to create roughness and form river training structures, providing benefit to macro- and microinvertebrate species.
Offshore rock mounds Placement of rock offshore from natural shorelines where shoreline protection is needed, typically where nearshore bathymetry is shallow or shorelines are poorly defined, or where direct placement of rock is difficult.
Boulder clusters Large stone placed slightly below base-flow water surface elevation to diversify flow regime and provide in-channel refuges for aquatic species.
Multiple round points Field of round stone structures, generally slightly emerged, used to create bathymetric and flow diversity.
Side channel hard point Stone structures placed at regular intervals along an eroding bank, which may be trenched in, keyed in, or dumped on shore to resist forces associated with bank failure.
Woody debris Placement of trees at least 25 ft long perpendicular or at an angle to the shoreline; trunks can be buried in shore sand or anchored using a rock key-in, usually in areas sheltered from wind-generated waves.
Spillway Section of embankment engineered to provide controlled overtopping of an embankment system, typically at the downstream end of a site at an elevation lower than the exterior berm.
Diversion Structure intended to divert flow from the main channel, typically into a bypass or cut-off channel.
Other Other type of riverine flow structure; must be specified in the sdsFeatureDescription field.

RestorFeatStatus

A description of the status of the individual restoration. It also includes the party responsible for the individual feature.

Value Description
USACE Planned Feature is in a USACE planning stage.
USACE Built Feature has been built by USACE. If a feature has been planned and built by USACE, once constructed, it will always be considered USACE built even if repaired later.
USACE Enhanced The function of an existing feature not built by USACE has been enhanced by a feature that was USACE built or repaired.
USACE Repaired An existing feature not built by USACE has been directly improved or repaired.
Other Planned Feature planned by another organization or government agency.
Other Built Feature built by another organization or government agency and is an integral part of the UMRR project.
Other Enhanced Function of an existing feature built by another organization or government agency has been enhanced by a feature built or repaired by a non-USACE organization or government agency.
Other Repaired Existing feature directly improved or repaired by another organization or government agency.

Value Description

Awash Flush with or washed by waves at low water under average meteorological conditions.
CoversAndUncovers Projection from the bottom that periodically extends above and is submerged below the water surface.
Floating Resting or moving on the surface of a water body.
PartlySubmergedAtHighWater Partially covered and partially dry at high water.
SubjectToInundationOrFlooding Area or structure periodically covered by flood waters.
Unknown Relationship to water level is unknown.
